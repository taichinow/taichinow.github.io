The Master Command Architecture (Tâm Ý Truyền Lệnh)

In Chen Xin's internal martial system, the human nervous-energetic axis is organized hierarchically like an imperial army command staff:

1. Tướng Quân Tâm (Heart General)

The Heart houses the conscious spirit (*Shen*). It does not move physically, but serves as Sovereign Commander.

2. Ý Truyền Lệnh (Intent Messenger)

Intent (*Yi*) is the transmission mechanism (neuro-fascial signal) relaying commands down the spine to Dantian.

Dantian as the Central Garrison (Trại Binh Mã)

Chen Xin specifically compares the Lower Dantian to an army encampment (*Như binh mã đồn xứ*). Qi gathers here in stillness and dispatches outward in movement. When Qi returns to Dantian (*Toàn thể chi khí quy túc xứ*), the whole body remains rooted and recharged.

Thanh Khí Thượng Thăng (Pure Qi Ascends)

Light, refined energetic intent ascends through shoulders, elbows, and wrists to the fingertips for martial sensitivity and release (*Lu*, *Na*, *Fa-jin*).

Trọc Khí Hạ Giáng (Turbid Qi Descends)

Heavy, grounding gravitational force drops through hips, knees, and ankles down into the heels and bubbling wells (*Yongquan*) for indestructible root.

The Principle of Spherical Completeness (Toàn Thân Thái Cực)

Chen Xin describes how a practitioner must visualize and embody the Taiji circle internally and externally:

1. No Bulges, No Hollows (Bất khuyết bất hàm)

The circle represents the ideal energetic bubble. If any part of the body pushes out rigidly (bulge) or collapses flaccidly (hollow), the circle breaks and opponent force can penetrate.

2. Stillness is Wuji · Movement is Taiji

Before moving, the practitioner stands in *Wuji* (emptiness, undifferentiated potential). The moment intention begins to move, Yin and Yang differentiate: full and empty, opening and closing (*Khai - Hợp*), ascending and descending.

3. Alignment of the Central Channel

The plumb-line from *Baihui* (crown) down through *Huiyin* (pelvic floor) must remain straight and suspended throughout every twist and turn. The waist rotates around this axis like a wheel on an axle (*Lập như bình chuẩn, hoạt tự xa luân*).

太極拳勢圖 — 12 Anatomical & Energetic Posture Rules of Chen Xin

Chen Xin's detailed instructions for the single-leg rooted posture (Kim Kê Độc Lập / Độc lập bộ).

Rule 1 頂精領住
Đỉnh Tinh Lĩnh Trụ (Head Suspended)

Crown of the head suspended lightly from above as if tied with a thread. Never stiffen the neck; keep cervical vertebrae open and decompressed.

Rule 2 耳聽身後
Nhĩ Thính Thân Hậu (Listen Behind)

Ears actively listening behind the body. Cultivates 360-degree spatial and situational awareness (*Thần bao quát chu thân*).

Rule 3 眼看右脚
Nhãn Khán Hữu Cước (Eyes on Right Foot)

Gaze directed down toward the raised right foot, integrating head orientation with the lower diagonal strike/deflection vector.

Rule 4 左肘屈住
Tả Trửu Khuất Trụ (Left Elbow Bent)

Left elbow must remain flexed and rounded, maintaining spring-like internal ward-off (*Peng Jin*) without locking or hyperextending.

Rule 5 肩鬆下
Kiên Tùng Hạ (Shoulders Sunk)

Shoulders sink completely downward (*Trầm kiên trụy trửu*), preventing Qi from rising up to the chest and ensuring seamless torque transfer.

Rule 6 右肱伸開
Hữu Quăng Thân Khai (Right Arm Open)

The right upper arm extends and opens broadly, maintaining structural expansion without collapsing inward against the ribs.

Rule 7 左右掌朝下
Tả Hữu Chưởng Triều Hạ (Palms Face Down)

Both palms face downward toward the earth, establishing a downward grounding and pressing energy (*Án kình / Chìm đè*).

Rule 8 腰剉下去
Yêu Tỏa Hạ Khứ (Waist Sinks Down)

The lumbar curve and inguinal crease (*Kua*) sink deeply, grounding the pelvis and stabilizing the body's center of mass.

Rule 9 左腿彎不可軟
Tả Thoái Loan Bất Khả Nhuyễn (Left Leg Firm)

The supporting left leg is bent but resiliently elastic. It must never collapse into weakness or limp passivity.

Rule 10 右脚與腿平膝屈
Hữu Cước Dữ Thoái Bình (Right Foot Level)

The lifted right foot is held level with the thigh with knee bent, ready to instantly convert into a front stomp, side sweep, or low root.

Rule 11 左膝屈二三分
Tả Tất Khuất Nhị Tam Phân (Knee 20-30%)

The supporting left knee is bent only 20–30% (2 to 3 tenths). Bending too deeply destroys mobility; locking it breaks root.

Rule 12 左足週身力量當
Tả Túc Chu Thân Đương (100% Rooted)

The entire weight and dynamic tension of the body is cleanly borne by the supporting left foot, achieving absolute distinction between Full and Empty (*Hư Thực phân minh*).

Figure 13 · Hình 13

"When moving it separates, when resting it unites"

動之則分，靜之則合 · Động thì phân ra, tịnh thì hợp lại

Biomechanical Translation of Wang Zongyue's Classic Formula

"太極者，無極而生，動靜之機，陰陽之母也。動之則分，靜之則合。"
"Thái cực giả, vô cực nhi sinh, động tĩnh chi cơ, âm dương chi mẫu dã. Động thì phân ra, tịnh thì hợp lại."
1. Centrifugal Expansion · Động Phân:

When the torso initiates rotation around the vertical spine axis, rotational kinetic energy generates centrifugal force. Both arms swing outward in expanding arcs. This is the expansive, release phase (*Khai* / *Fa-jin*).

2. Centripetal Convergence · Tịnh Hợp:

As rotation slows or arrives at conclusion, the limbs fold inward toward the central trunk via centripetal attraction. Energy gathers back into Dantian for dynamic stability (*Hợp* / *Tụ khí*).

3. S-Curve Spiral Geometry (Bird's-Eye View B):

Looking down from above, the opposing arm trajectories form an interlocking S-curve. This proves that Taiji motion is never linear or square, but continuous helical silk-reeling (*Triển ty kình*).
