What the Daozang is

Đạo Tạng (道藏) — in Chinese Daozang — is the name given to the collected scriptures of Daoism, gathered, classified and edited over many dynasties. Several canons were compiled in Chinese history, but the only one to survive intact is the Ming woodblock edition: the 《正統道藏》 Zhengtong Daozang (1445) together with its continuation, the 《萬曆續道藏》 Wanli Xu Daozang (1607).

So when people say "the Daozang" they usually mean the combined printing of these two: 5,485 juan (卷) in 512 cases (函), containing close to 1,500 separate texts. The set is numbered using the 《千字文》 Thousand Character Classic — each case carries one character from that ancient poem, an ingenious way of "encoding a library".

The edition in circulation today descends from the early twentieth century: after the Ming printing blocks were destroyed in the Boxer upheaval, Shanghai's Hanfen Lou (上海涵芬樓) photographed and reprinted the copy preserved at the White Cloud Abbey (白雲觀) in Beijing — which is how the canon reached us at all.

Structure: San Dong · Si Fu · Shi Er Lei

The whole canon is organised by the system 三洞四輔十二類 — Three Caverns, Four Supplements, Twelve Categories. This is not merely shelving: it is a theological map, in which each tier corresponds to a grade of teaching and a distinct line of transmission.

三洞 · Three Caverns

Three "caverns" — three tiers of teaching

洞真 Dongzhen — the Shangqing 上清 scriptures: celestial realms, spiritual cultivation.
洞玄 Dongxuan — the Lingbao 靈寶 scriptures: liturgy, ritual, universal salvation.
洞神 Dongshen — the Sanhuang 三皇 scriptures: talismans, exorcism, healing.

Three sections, each divided into 12 categories →
 四輔 · Four Supplements

Four supporting sections

太玄 Taixuan supports Dongzhen · 太平 Taiping supports Dongxuan · 太清 Taiqing supports Dongshen · 正一 Zhengyi runs through all three (the Celestial Master line, 天師道).

 十二類 · Twelve Categories

Twelve categories of text

Every section of the Caverns and Supplements is subdivided by twelve genres — from Bản Văn / 本文 (root scripture) and 神符 (talismans) to 玉訣 (commentary) and 表奏 (memorials).

洞神 Dongshen Sanhuang 三皇 scriptures — talismans, exorcism, healing, ritual technique Sanhuang school; Celestial Master Daoism 天師道

The word dong (洞) means "to penetrate" or "to be thoroughly conversant with" — it denotes the practitioner's penetration of truth, not a physical cave.

Compilation history

The Daozang was not written once. It is the result of more than a thousand years of accumulation — and each compilation was tied to a dynasty, an emperor, and the shifting political contest between Daoism and Buddhism.

The principal editions

Ming · 1445 + 1607 · the essential one

正統道藏 & 萬曆續道藏

The only complete surviving ancient canon. 5,485 juan in 512 cases. The most widely used modern reprint is the 36-volume set from 文物出版社 / 上海書店 / 天津古籍出版社, 1988.

5,485 juan · 512 cases · ~1,476 texts
 Qing · 1906 · anthology

重刊道藏輯要 — Daozang Jiyao

The Qing never cut a full canon, only a selection of essentials. Recut in 1906 at the Erxian An 二仙庵 in Chengdu — arranged in 28 volumes named after the twenty-eight lunar mansions (角亢氐房心尾箕…軫).

28 volumes · after the 二十八宿
 Taiwan · 1950s–70s · internal alchemy

道藏精華 — Daozang Jinghua

An anthology compiled in Taiwan by Xiao Tianshi 蕭天石, concentrating on internal alchemy 內丹 and health cultivation — the portion closest to the concerns of a taijiquan practitioner.

73 volumes · neidan focus
 PRC · 2004 · modern

中華道藏 — Zhonghua Daozang

Produced under the Chinese Daoist Association, 48 volumes. Its great advantage is modern punctuation, plus the inclusion of Dunhuang 敦煌 and newly recovered texts.

48 volumes · punctuated
 1990s · beyond the canon

藏外道書 · 敦煌道藏 · 道書集成

Collections of scriptures lying outside the Ming canon — recovering texts that were lost, or were never admitted to the orthodox collection.

Extends beyond the 5,485 juan
 Scholarship · English · 2004

The Taoist Canon — Schipper & Verellen

A three-volume reference work (not the texts themselves) by Kristofer Schipper and Franciscus Verellen, analysing and locating all 1,476 texts of the canon — the international scholarly standard.

3 vols · University of Chicago Press

Why the Daozang matters for taijiquan

The Daozang is the documentary source of nearly the whole theoretical apparatus that taijiquan inherits: the concepts of qi 氣, the dantian 丹田, the microcosmic orbit 周天, yin and yang 陰陽, wu wei 無為, and the union of Heaven and humanity 天人合一 — all argued out in the scriptures of the Taiqing and Dongshen sections.

When a taijiquan teacher says "use intent, not force" (用意不用力), or "let the qi sink to the dantian" (氣沉丹田), those are fragments of a larger intellectual system — and the Daozang is where that system is recorded most completely.
