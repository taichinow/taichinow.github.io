2. Notes Relevant to the Description of the Postures

(Những Điểm Liên Quan đến Phần Thuyết Minh Động Tác) 2.1. This form is a two-person paired routine for the practice of Taijiquan combat techniques. Its movements draw together the famous techniques of the five major Taijiquan schools — Chen (陈), Yang (杨), Wu (吴), Wu (武) and Sun (孙). The movements of this paired routine not only strengthen the body and build health; they also embody the offensive and defensive technical characteristics of the celebrated solo routines of the various Taijiquan schools. This helps practitioners who have already trained the solo routines to deepen their appreciation and grasp the practical value of every technique within a real combat routine.

2.2. In the description of the postures, the explanations follow the order of the movements or of the technical-combat requirements of each movement. The illustrations use arrows to mark the direction of each practitioner's motion, together with drawings of the limb movements, so that the reader may form a clear three-dimensional picture of the spatial relationships of the movements. The two illustrations A and B represent the following: A's face points south, B's face points north; although their orientations differ, the figures are drawn to the same scale, and the sizes of the two figures are therefore described in proportion. Where one figure is obscured by the other, the movements are explained in a decomposed manner, and the diagrams strive to keep the two figures clearly contrasted with one another.

1. Commencement (Khởi Thức)

1/ A faces south, B faces north; the front-to-back distance between the two is about one thước three, the diagonal distance about two and a half thước. Stand naturally, legs slightly bent, arms hanging loosely at the sides, palms resting against the outer thighs. Eyes look straight forward, breathing natural. Heels together, toes turned slightly outward to form the character 八 "eight" (hình 1).

2/ A and B lift the right toes, pivot on the heel rotating inward, and set the foot down. At the same time the body turns slightly to the left, the two now facing each other (hình 2).

Key point: The muscles of the whole body relax, head and neck held upright, chin slightly drawn in; do not deliberately thrust out the chest or hollow the belly.

2. Lazy Tying Clothes (Lan Trá Tỳ)

1/ A and B slowly raise both arms, elbows slightly bent, thumbs pointing upward, hands at shoulder height and shoulder-width apart, palms facing one another. As the movement completes, the weight shifts slightly onto the right leg and the left heel lifts (hình 3).

2/ Continuing from the previous posture, sink the shoulders and drop the elbows, relax the waist and settle the hips, the body sinks slightly, both arms slightly curved, slowly carrying forward to the front of the abdomen. At the same time the left leg steps slowly forward, heel touching the ground (hình 4).

As the left leg steps forward, both hands slowly lift and extend forward, arms slightly curved, palms facing one another, eyes looking forward between the two hands. The body's weight continues moving forward, the left foot presses flat, the right foot follows up to about an inch behind the left, ball of the foot touching the ground; at the same time the left foot also gradually settles fully (hình 5).

3/ Continuing from the previous posture, the weight shifts to the rear, the right foot presses flat, the left toes lift, both hands remain unchanged (hình 6).

4/ The body turns to the right (A and B now back to back), both arms bend slightly and lift level with the chest, moving rightward with the body's rotation. The right hand rotates outward (palm facing up), the left hand rotates inward (palm facing down), the tips of the left index and middle fingers lightly resting against the inner side of the right arm. At the same time the left foot pivots on its heel, turning right. Eyes follow the right hand (hình 7).

5/ The right arm bends slightly, right palm facing up, sweeping from the right side rearward in an arc of about half a circle, palm still facing up; the back of the left hand faces up and remains resting against the inner side of the right arm, travelling together with the right hand. The hands arrive just in front of the right shoulder, about an inch from it; A now faces west, B faces east (hình 8).

6/ Without pausing, the above movement continues: both hands push out toward the right front (right hand leading), both arms slightly curved. At the same time the right toes lift and rotate to the right, then the whole foot leaves the ground and turns toward the right……

……stepping out to the right side, the heel touching the ground first and then gradually pressing flat. The left foot follows up, the weight moving forward to come to rest about an inch behind the right foot, ball of the foot touching the ground. Eyes follow the right hand (hình 9).

3. Opening-Closing Form (Khai Thức Hợp Thức)

1/ Both A and B keep the left foot pressed flat where it is, the right toes turning slightly inward to point straight ahead; then the left heel lifts and the body turns to face directly forward (hình 10).

……At the same time both hands follow the turning of the body until the two palms face one another (about two inches apart). Eyes look straight ahead.

2/ Body and both legs stationary; the two palms, still facing, separate to left and right to shoulder width, fingers pointing upward, relaxed naturally, with a brief pause. This is the Opening (khai thức).

3/ Both hands slowly draw together again to the former position, with a brief pause. This is the Closing (hợp thức). A faces due south, B faces due north (hình 10).

4. Single Whip (Đơn Tiên Thức)

1/ A and B lightly lift the left foot and step one pace sideways to the left, the body's weight moving onto the left leg, the left leg bending at the knee into a "horizontal-crotch stance" (hoành đương bộ). At the same time both arms rotate slightly outward and the palms separate to left and right, palms facing outward. The head turns left, eyes on the partner, the two practitioners' palms about an inch apart (hình 11).

10. Right Crouching Tiger (Hữu Phục Hổ) — B

1/ As A is about to bump into him, B turns the body to the right, weight moving rearward, left knee slightly bent, right leg straightening, right toes lifting, escaping A's bump. At the same time the left hand bends at the elbow and arcs down before the body, seizing A's left elbow and plucking (thái) it down and to the left. The right arm bends at the elbow, the right fist withdrawing to the inner side of the left arm. Eyes on A (hình 23).

2/ The right foot lifts and arcs around behind A's left foot, the right leg bending at the knee and the left leg straightening into a right bow stance. At the same time the right fist withdraws to the waist, the right arm rotating inward and drawing back, bending at the elbow, then arcing forward and upward to strike……

……striking toward A's upper back. Fist-heart facing down, eyes on A (hình 24).

Key point: After the right fist withdraws, one must hollow the chest, loosen the hips and turn the waist — the movement must be carried through in one flowing action. The left hand plucking A's left arm, the right fist striking out and the bow-stance footwork must all be coordinated in rhythm with one another.

11. Left Topping Elbow (Tả Đỉnh Chẩu) — A

As B's fist is about to strike the upper back, A's right hand arcs down from the right side to before the abdomen and then up again (palm facing up), seizing B's left wrist; the movement is continuous without pause (arcing upward while rotating the arm inward), plucking (thái) B's hand up and to the right. The left hand frees itself from B's left hand, bends at the elbow and forms a fist, striking out sideways, fist-heart facing down (using the elbow point to strike into B's chest).

At the same time both legs bend at the knees and sink. Eyes on B (hình 25).

Key point: When sinking, the buttocks must not protrude. The plucking (thái) of the hand, the elbow strike of the left arm (đỉnh chẩu) and the sinking of both legs must all be carried out at the same instant.

12. Right Retreating Elbow (Hữu Thối Chẩu) — B

As A's left elbow is about to strike the chest, B hollows the chest and raises the back, the left leg bending at the knee, the waist turning left, weight moving rearward, neutralising this elbow strike. 13. Left Side-sweeping-body Fist (Tả Phiết Thân Chuy) — A

A takes advantage of B's pushing pressure on the elbow, the body turning right to neutralise B's push, then rising with the momentum, the body leading the waist as it turns back to the left. The left leg bends at the knee and the right leg straightens into a left……

……bow stance. At the same time the waist turns left, using the back of the fist to sweep sideways into B's face (phiết thân chuy), fist-heart facing up. The right hand bends at the elbow and rests against the inner side of the left arm, palm slanting downward. Eyes on B (hình 27).

Key point: The side-sweeping-body fist must travel in an arc; the arm must not straighten. Eyes follow the left fist. The sinking and the bow stance must be performed at the same time.
