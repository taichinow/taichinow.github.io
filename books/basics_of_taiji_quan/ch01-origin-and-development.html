Chương 1: Origin and Development of Taiji Quan

Basics of Taiji Quan — Li Xingdong · Foreign Languages Press Beijing

Taiji Quan is a profound treasure of ancient Chinese cultural heritage, harmoniously crystallizing eastern cosmological philosophy and sophisticated internal martial application. Its deepest philosophical roots trace directly to the Yin-Yang doctrine and the I Ching (Book of Changes), which perceive all cosmic phenomena as an eternal, fluid dance between opposing complementary forces: soft and hard, dynamic and quiescent, empty and solid.

Popular historical tradition attributes the foundation of internal martial arts to the legendary Daoist master Zhang Sanfeng on Mount Wudang. Observing a contest between a white crane and a snake, he witnessed how the serpent's soft, coiled yielding neutralized the fierce, direct lunges of the crane's beak, inspiring circular, continuous movements that overcome hard power with yielding softness.

Modern historical research identifies General Chen Wangting (1600–1680) of Chenjiagou (Chen Village), Wenxian County, Henan Province, as the pivotal figure who systematized existing martial techniques, integrating traditional Chinese medicine meridian theory, Daoyin gymnastic stretches, and abdominal breathing to establish Taiji Quan.
