## Chapter 6: Peng (Phòng) — Ward Off: the sphere of organized readiness

| 🇺🇸 English |
|-------------|----------------|
| **The big idea**: Peng (Phòng / 棚) is the foundational energy of Taichi. It is not a "block" or a "ward off" in the karate sense. It is a *sphere of organized readiness* around the body. Imagine an invisible ball of pressurized air that surrounds you. When something presses in, the sphere pushes back — not with muscular force, but with the integrated structure of bones, joints, and connective tissue. |
| **Why Peng is "expanded" but not "stiff"**: A common mistake is to interpret Peng as "puffed up" or "holding tension." True Peng is *biao* (表 / biǎo — "outward" but bouncy), like a basketball that gives when pressed but springs back. If you stand in Wuji (Vô Cực) stance and have a partner push on your shoulder, you should feel the energy go from their hand through your arm into your foot and back. That's Peng. |
| **The 3.0 practice of Peng**: |
| **1. The "Balloon" Visualization** (Hình ảnh quả bóng bay): Stand in Bì Bộ, arms slightly forward as if holding a large beach ball. The ball is 18 inches in front of you, slightly larger than your body. Don't squeeze it; just keep it. Feel the "spherical" energy in your hands and arms. Breathe 10 breaths. |
| **2. The "Wrist Float"** (Cổ tay nổi): Stand normally, hold arms out in front, palms down, elbows slightly bent. Have a partner place a finger under your wrist and press up gently. The wrist should float up under light pressure, not collapse. That's Peng in the wrist. |
| **3. The "Tree" Practice** (Tập Cây): In Zhan Zhuang (Chapter 20-21), hold the rounded arm position and imagine your arms as branches — flexible, not rigid. The wind (life) can move them, but they always return to the structure. |
| **Why 50+ needs Peng**: As we age, we unconsciously "close down" — hunching, tightening, becoming smaller. Peng is the opposite. It is *open, expanded, ready*. The practice of Peng teaches the 50+ body to expand again, to claim space, to *be present* in the world. This is not just physical — it is psychological. People who have lost Peng look old. People who have it look alive. |
