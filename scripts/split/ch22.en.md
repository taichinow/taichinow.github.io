## Chapter 22: Why slow is fast (and slow is real)

| 🇺🇸 English |
|-------------|----------------|
| **The big idea**: Slow is not "easy." Slow is *revealing*. When you move fast, you can't see your mistakes. When you move slowly, every error becomes obvious. The slow Taichi form is *the diagnostic*. It shows you where you're stiff, where you're rushed, where you're holding tension, where you're not breathing. Slow is the *fastest* way to learn, because it makes every mistake a teacher. |
| **The "10x slower" rule**: When you think you're going "slow," go 10x slower. A 4-minute form done in 40 minutes is *more* learning than a 4-minute form done in 4 minutes. Why? Because at 40 minutes, you have time to *notice*. You notice the shoulder that rises, the breath that holds, the foot that lands heavy. These notices are the seeds of change. |
| **The "fragmentation" technique**: Take a single form (e.g., "White Crane Spreads Its Wings" / Bạch Hạc Lượng Xích). Do just the *transition into* the posture for 5 minutes. Then just the *posture itself* for 5 minutes. Then just the *transition out* for 5 minutes. This is 15 minutes on a 5-second form. It is *the* way to find the subtlety. |
| **The "30-minute minimum" rule**: The slow form needs at least 30 minutes to *teach* anything. Why? Because the first 10 minutes are just "warming up" — getting out of the day's stress. The next 10 minutes are "settling" — the body starts to find the form. The last 10 minutes are *where learning happens*. A 10-minute form practice is *not enough*. |
| **Why 50+ benefits most from slow**: The 50+ body is *already* slow in the wrong ways — slow to react, slow to recover, slow to learn new patterns. Taichi's slow practice takes that natural slowness and *redirects* it. Instead of "slow because aging," you become "slow because precise." This reframe is *profound*. The 50+ Taichi practitioner is not "behind" the 25-year-old — they are on a *different* path that values depth over speed. |
