## Chapter 5: The "Wu Wei" Stance (Vô Vi) and the Standing Post (Trụ Thế)

| 🇺🇸 English |
|-------------|----------------|
| **The big idea**: "Wu Wei" (Vô Vi / 無為) is often translated "do nothing" or "effortless action," but the deeper meaning is "act without forcing." In Taichi, this is the **Standing Post (Trụ Thế / 站樁)**: standing still, with proper structure, for 5-20 minutes daily. It looks like nothing. It is the *most important* Taichi practice. |
| **Why standing is everything**: All 24 form movements are the standing posture in motion. If your standing posture is wrong (shoulders up, hips tilted, weight on heels, knees locked), then every form movement will carry those errors. If you fix the standing posture first, the form takes care of itself. The masters say: "One year of standing, ten years of form." |
| **The 4 stances of Taichi** (4 tư thế cơ bản): |
| **1. Bì Bộ (平行步) — Parallel Stance** (feet parallel, hip-width): The "neutral" stance. Use for: warm-up, cool-down, balance drills, most basic standing practice. |
| **2. Dị Bộ (丁字步) — T-Stance** (one foot forward, heel-to-heel in line, T-shape): The "transition" stance between forward and back. Use for: form transition practice, balance refinement. |
| **3. Cung Bộ (弓步) — Bow Stance** (front leg bent at 90° like a drawn bow, back leg straight): The "attack" stance. Use for: form postures, real-world applications, lower body strength. |
| **4. Hư Bộ (虛步) — Empty Stance** (front foot just touching, 90% weight on back leg): The "ready" stance, weight ready to shift. Use for: form transitions, push-hands practice. |
| **The "5 adjustments" of Standing Post** (Ngũ Điều Chỉnh — applied to ANY stance): |
| 1. **Hư Linh Đỉnh Kình** (虛靈頂勁) — Crown of head lifts gently. Imaginary string pulling up. |
| 2. **Hàm Hung Bạt Bối** (含胸拔背) — Chest slightly hollow, back slightly rounded/open. Not military posture. |
| 3. **Trầm Khi Trạch Đan** (沉氣鬆肩) — Shoulders sink and relax. Elbows drop. |
| 4. **Hạ Nguyên Thất Phù** (下頦斂收) — Chin slightly tucked. Back of neck long. |
| 5. **Tọa Giác Hướng Hạ** (坐角向胯) — Sit into the hips. Pelvis tilts slightly forward. Knees over toes (not past). |
| **The 3.0 starting protocol**: |
| Week 1-2: 3 minutes, Bì Bộ (parallel), focus on breath. |
| Week 3-4: 5 minutes, add "5 adjustments" focus. |
| Week 5-8: 8 minutes, add Dantian focus (Chapter 14). |
| Week 9-12: 10 minutes, add shoulder relaxation scan. |
| After 3 months: 15-20 minutes, add structured variation (alternate Bì Bộ and Dị Bộ). |
