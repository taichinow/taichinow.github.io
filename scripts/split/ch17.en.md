## Chapter 17: Distinguish Sōng (松) from Sūi (Sụp đổ)

| 🇺🇸 English |
|-------------|----------------|
| **The big idea**: Sōng (松) is *relaxed structure*. Sūi (Tụy / 墜 — sometimes written 隨 or 垂) is *collapse*. They look similar but are completely different. Sōng: the body is like a ball of pressurized air — full but soft. Sūi: the body is like a deflated balloon — empty and floppy. Sōng has *Peng* at its core. Sūi has no Peng. |
| **The "Peng test" for Sōng**: If you stand and a partner pushes on your shoulder: (a) If you push back with the same force they used, you're stiff (not Sōng). (b) If you collapse, you're Sūi. (c) If you resist with the same force BUT it comes from structure (not muscular tension), you're Sōng. The 50+ practitioner who has Sōng *feels* bouncy. The one with Sūi *feels* deflated. |
| **The "50+ collapse" pattern**: After 50, the body defaults to one of two patterns: (1) "Stiff rigidity" — locked shoulders, locked knees, holding-on pattern. (2) "Collapsed surrender" — slumped chest, no Peng, no structure. Both are *exhausting*. Sōng is the *third option*: structure that moves, energy that flows, rest that doesn't sag. |
| **The 3.0 practice**: Stand in Bì Bộ. First, *intentionally* collapse — let your chest cave, your head drop, your arms hang heavy. Notice how this *feels*. Now, *intentionally* stiffen — lock your knees, raise your shoulders, hold your arms stiff. Notice how this feels. Now, *intentionally* find Sōng: structure but no tension, soft but not collapsed. This is the *calibration* practice — learn to feel the difference. |
