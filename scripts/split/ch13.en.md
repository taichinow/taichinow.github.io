## Chapter 13: Substantial and Insubstantial stepping (Tấn Hư Tấn Thực)

| 🇺🇸 English |
|-------------|----------------|
| **The big idea**: Every step in Taichi is a *Hư → Thực* transition. The foot leaves the ground as Hư (light, empty), travels through space, lands as Hư (still light, not yet full weight), and *only then* becomes Thực as the weight shifts onto it. Most beginners skip the "still light after landing" phase. They step and immediately load the new leg. This makes the step look heavy, choppy, and disconnected from the breath. |
| **The 4 phases of a Taichi step**: |
| **1. Lift (Nâng)**: Toe of back foot lifts, heel stays on ground briefly. Foot is Hư. |
| **2. Move (Chuyển)**: Foot lifts off ground, moves through space. Foot is Hư (zero weight). |
| **3. Place (Đặt)**: Foot lands. The heel touches first (for forward step), or the toe first (for back step). The foot is *still* Hư — no weight yet. |
| **4. Settle (Lắng)**: The weight *gradually* shifts onto the new foot. It becomes Thực. The 5-second pause from Chapter 12 happens HERE. |
| **The common footwork error — "step and load"**: Beginner steps, then immediately puts full weight on the new foot. This skips phases 3 and 4. Result: the step is heavy, the breath is choppy, the body is "bouncing." The fix: pause *after* the foot lands, *before* the weight shifts. This pause is 1-2 seconds for normal practice, 5+ seconds for Hư/Thực training. |
| **The "vacuum foot" exercise** (Chân hút chân không): In Cung Bộ, focus on the back foot. The back foot is at 90% empty. Now slowly, *without losing balance*, lift the back foot just 1 inch off the ground. Hold 10 seconds. If you can do this without wobbling, your Hư/Thực is clean enough to do the form. If you wobble, your Hư/Thực needs more work. |
| **Why 50+ needs slow stepping**: Aging causes two problems: (1) loss of balance, (2) fear of falling. The 4-phase Taichi step *directly* addresses both. By pausing between steps, the body has time to *register* the new stable state. This retrains the vestibular system (inner ear balance) and the proprioceptive system (joint position sense). The result: better balance, less fear, more confidence. |
