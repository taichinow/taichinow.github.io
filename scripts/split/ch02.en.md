## Chapter 2: The Three Treasures (Tam Bảo) — Jing, Qi, Shen

| 🇺🇸 English |
|-------------|----------------|
| **The big idea**: Traditional Chinese medicine and Taichi share a model of human energy organized in three layers, from densest to most subtle: **Jing** (Tinh / 精) — the body's essential substance (fluids, hormones, bone marrow, deep vitality). **Qi** (Khí / 氣) — the vital energy that circulates and animates. **Shen** (Thần / 神) — the spirit, the mind, the consciousness that observes and directs. |
| **Why this matters for 3.0**: You don't need to believe in Qi to benefit from Taichi. But the *framework* is useful: every Taichi practice affects all three layers. (1) Standing meditation (Trụ Thế) cultivates **Jing** — deep bone-marrow vitality, joint health, hormonal balance. (2) Slow form with breath cultivates **Qi** — circulation, oxygenation, nervous system regulation. (3) Mindful attention cultivates **Shen** — focus, emotional regulation, clarity. |
| **The Three Treasures Hierarchy**: The classical relationship is: **Jing produces Qi, Qi produces Shen, Shen commands Jing**. In English: deep body vitality creates the energy to move; energy in motion creates the awareness to direct; awareness directs how you use your vitality. This is not mystical — it is a systems description. Sleep (Jing) → Energy (Qi) → Focus (Shen) → Smart choices about sleep again. A 50+ practitioner protects Jing first (don't burn the candle), builds Qi second (consistent practice), and refines Shen third (meditation). |
| **The 3.0 mistake to avoid**: Trying to "open the third eye" or "move Qi to my fingertips" in the first year. That is 5.0+ work, and chasing it leads to bad form. At 3.0, your job is to (1) protect your sleep (Jing), (2) breathe deeply and move slowly (Qi), (3) pay attention (Shen). Everything else comes later. |
| **The 50+ secret**: After 50, Jing naturally declines. The world tries to sell you hormones and supplements to fight it. Taichi's answer is different: protect what Jing you have (don't burn the candle with overwork, poor sleep, and stress), and let the practice rebuild the *quality* of your remaining Jing through slow, deep, joint-friendly movement. You cannot stop aging. You can decide *how* you age. |
