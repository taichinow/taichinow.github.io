## Chapter 4: The Body Schema — Weight, Ground, Alignment

| 🇺🇸 English |
|-------------|----------------|
| **The big idea**: Before any movement, your body must have three foundational relationships: (1) **Weight** (Trọng lượng) — feeling the body's mass settle naturally into the floor, not gripping. (2) **Ground** (Mặt đất) — the soles of the feet as the only contact with reality; everything else is above. (3) **Alignment** (Trục) — the head, shoulders, hips, knees, ankles stack over each other like beads on a string. |
| **Why "schema" matters**: A "schema" is a mental map of your body. Most 50+ people have a *damaged* schema — they think their shoulders are up by their ears (they aren't), or their weight is on their heels (it should be on the whole foot). Taichi rebuilds the schema by giving you slow, precise movements where you can *feel* what is actually true. |
| **The 4 weight checkpoints** (do this standing right now): |
| 1. **Big toe mound** (Mũi ngón chân cái) — should feel the floor. If not, the weight is too far back. |
| 2. **Little toe mound** (Mũi ngón út) — should also feel the floor. If not, weight is on the inside arch. |
| 3. **Inside heel** (Gót trong) — should feel the floor. The arch should be lifted, not collapsed. |
| 4. **Outside heel** (Gót ngoài) — should also feel the floor. The outer edge of the heel is the "anchor." |
| **The "Three Points" alignment** (Hư Linh Đỉnh Kình / 虛靈頂勁): A line drawn from the crown of your head to the floor between your feet. The crown lifts gently upward (Đỉnh kình); the tailbone drops gently downward. This *lengthens* the spine without forcing it. The chin tucks slightly, the back of the neck stays long. This is the central axis (Trục) of every Taichi posture. |
| **The 50+ lesson**: After 50, the body has decades of *habit* posture. Shoulders forward, head jutting out, hips tilted, knees locked. Taichi does not "fix" these — it gives the body a *new* pattern, and over months, the body slowly returns to the new pattern when not practicing. Be patient. A 30-year habit takes at least 1-2 years to soften, and that's normal. |
