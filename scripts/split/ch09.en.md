## Chapter 9: An (Án) — Push: grounding and finishing

| 🇺🇸 English |
|-------------|----------------|
| **The big idea**: An (Án / 按) is the energy of *pressing down and through*. After Ji presses forward, An *anchors* the energy. The energy goes down into the ground, not out into the air. In the 24-form, An appears in "Phân Chẩn" (Press Down / 掤擠按) and in the closing movements of many forms. The image: like a farmer pressing a seed into the soil — not pushing it through, but *planting* it. |
| **An has a "down and forward" quality**: Unlike Ji (which is purely forward), An has a *downward* component. The hand goes forward but slightly down, the elbow drops, the weight sinks, the foot roots. It's the "completing" energy — the energy that finishes what Ji started. |
| **The "fa jing" potential at the end of An**: When An is done *suddenly* with all the right conditions — full body integration, breath release, and a final "settle" — it produces *fa jing* (phát lực / 發勁), a short, focused burst of whole-body power. At 3.0, you are NOT trying to produce fa jing. You are just learning the *structure* that will eventually allow fa jing to emerge naturally. |
| **The 3.0 practice of An**: |
| **1. The "Planting" practice** (Gieo hạt): Stand in Cung Bộ. Hold one hand forward at chest height, palm down. Slowly press down and forward, as if pressing a seed 2 inches into the soil. Notice: the hand doesn't "push" the seed; it *guides* the energy into the ground. The other hand, in Peng position, opens to balance. |
| **2. The "Settle" practice** (Lắng): After a Ji press, instead of pulling the hand back, let it *settle* — drop slightly, the elbow releases, the shoulder drops, the body weight sinks. This is the "completion" of An. |
| **3. The "Down-breath" synchronization** (Đồng bộ thở xuống): As you press down with An, breathe out. As you release, breathe in. The breath and the press are *one* — not two separate things. |
| **The "closing" of the 8 Gates**: Peng, Lu, Ji, An are the first four of the 8 Gates (Bát Môn). The remaining four — Cai (採 / 採挒 / 採 / Pluck), Lieh (Liek / 挒 / Split), Chou (肘 / Elbow), and Kao (靠 / Bump / Shoulder) — are advanced and not for 3.0. The first four are *your* gates for the next 1-2 years. Master them. |
