The Waist Program — 12 Weeks

A complete protocol for “relax the waist, turn from the kua”: screening, six baseline tests, three phases, fourteen drills, three gates. Adults 50+, level 3.5 → 4.0.

Body MechanicsHead & NeckSpineThoraxShoulder GirdleElbow & WristWaist & PelvisKua & KneeFoot & AnkleThe Waist Program

Goal & Audience

This is not “relax more”. It teaches four separable skills in a fixed order, each with its own test.

Audience: adults over 50 who know the form, are at level 3.5 and want 4.0. Rhythm: 3 sessions/week (40–45 min) + 8 min/day.

Six measurable outcomes at week 12 (all six, or the block is unfinished):

  1. Hip hinge to 45° with a neutral spine, 8 clean reps, no knee pain.
  2. 10 controlled 45° turns each side, feet fixed, pelvis as one block, tailbone centred.
  3. Brush Knee ×6 with the waist initiating before the hands in ≥ 5 of 6.
  4. Single Whip led by the waist, no hand-only movement.
  5. 5 minutes of continuous form without breath-holding or heel lift.
  6. Single-leg stand 10 s each side, unsupported.

Four Skills, One Order

#SkillCharacterWhat it isFails as
S1Release鬆Unnecessary tension off, structure keptCollapse (塌)
S2Hinge折胯Fold at the hip joints, spine neutralBending the lower back
S3Turn轉腰Pelvis + torso rotate as one block on a hipTwisting the lumbar spine
S4Transmit主宰於腰Motion starts at the waist before the handsHands move, waist doesn't
Why this order: every source in the library puts release before turning (Fu: “only if the waist can relax will the two feet have strength”). Teaching S3 before S2 is the commonest way this fails in older students — they rotate the lumbar spine instead of the hips, and the back complains within three weeks.

Safety Screen

Do not start (refer first) with: acute low-back pain or sciatica (numbness, weakness, pain below the knee); spinal surgery within 6 months; diagnosed severe osteoporosis; uncontrolled hypertension; vertigo or unexplained falls in 3 months; an inflamed knee (swelling, heat, locking).

Stop the session on: sharp joint pain, numbness, dizziness, chest pain, or headache with breath-holding.

Standing ruleDetail
SupportChair or wall within reach in every standing drill until gate 2
Knee directionKnee over the second toe, never collapsing inward
DepthPhase A: knee flexion not past 90°; no low stances before phase C
SurfaceNon-slip; bare feet or thin flat shoes; never a soft mat
Pain ruleDiscomfort that fades on the exhale is fine; pain that grows is not

Evidence you may quote (from the research note, not re-verified): Tai Chi reduces falls — Cochrane 2019 RR 0.81 (95% CI 0.67–0.99); Li et al. 2005 (38 vs 73 falls over 6 months). Say “reduces falls”, not “because the qi sinks”.

Gate 2 (end of week 8)

What You May Claim

ClaimStatus
“The classics put the waist at the centre”Yes — 主宰於腰, 命意源頭在腰隙, 腰為一身之主宰 (five witnesses in the library)
“Ten Essentials item 3 says relax the waist”Yes — Fu Zhongwen pp. 268–269
“Wu Chien-chuan wrote ‘relax the waist; make it light and loose’”Only as quoted by Huang p. 26 — say “Huang 1993, p. 26, quoting…”
“The waist governs the pelvis and hip joints”Huang's reading, not the classical wording
“Relaxing the waist makes the qi sink”Traditional language. Chen Xin himself warns against pressing the qi down
“This program reduces falls”Yes for the program (Cochrane RR 0.81; Li 2005), not for the waist alone
“Science proves the waist leads movement”No. Rotation is measurable; the leading role has not been isolated

A training document, not medical advice. Screen per the Safety Screen section and stop on any red flag.

The Line to Remember

“Three months, one joint, one sentence: hips turn, waist follows.”
