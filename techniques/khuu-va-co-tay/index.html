Elbow & Wrist — Drop the Elbow, Seat the Wrist

Region 5: elbow and wrist. Why a locked elbow breaks the force chain, and how to keep the wrist as the body's shock absorber.

Body MechanicsHead & NeckSpineThoraxShoulder GirdleElbow & WristWaist & PelvisKua & KneeFoot & AnkleThe Waist Program

Structure

The elbow has only two motions: flexion-extension and forearm rotation. The wrist is the opposite — many small bones in layers, many joints, which is exactly why it works as the shock absorber of the whole arm chain.

In the short form, when the elbow is bent and the forearm vertical, the wrist sits level with the shoulder joint. Upper arm, forearm and the shoulder-to-wrist distance then form an equilateral triangle. Treat that triangle as a fixed frame and jìn expresses easily at the wrist or hand.

This is the application of “seek the straight within the curved”: do not straighten to gain force; keep the curve so force can pass.

Principle

墜肘 — elbow drops; 坐腕 — the wrist “sits”; 舒指 — fingers open, not clenched.

“The two elbows should hang loosely downward, the two arms naturally bent. This is the meaning of ‘seeking the straight within the curved, storing and then issuing’.” — Chen Xin, p. 659

“The five fingers are spread, the wrist sunk (塌腕), the tiger's mouth slightly rounded, the palm slightly drawn in, as though grasping and holding a ball.” — Chen Xin, p. 659

Sink, seat, open — three words that travel together. Without “open” the hand becomes a claw; without “seat” the force jams at the wrist.

Common Faults

FaultSignCorrection
Locked elbowElbow straight, force breaks mid-armKeep 10–15° of bend, always; never lock
Elbow liftedElbow level with or above the wristDrop the elbow first, then raise the arm
Wrist bentHand flexed more than 20°“Seat” it: push the heel of the hand forward, wrist straight
Stiff splayed fingersFingers rigid, feel like sticksCurve slightly, as if holding a small bird
GrippingTiger's mouth closed, nail beds whiteRound the tiger's mouth; force at the base of the fingers

Drills

DrillHowDosage
1. 90° triangleArm forward, elbow at 90°, wrist at shoulder height. An observer checks the three sides.Hold 30 s × 3
2. Seat the wristReach forward, flex the wrist fully, then push the heel of the hand forward until the wrist is straight. Hold 3 s.8 reps
3. Hold the birdHand as if holding a small bird that must neither escape nor be crushed. Hold 1 minute.1 min
4. Wall pressPalm on the wall, press gently. Feel the chain: hand → wrist → elbow → shoulder → back.5 × 5 s

Cues & Gate

Cue: “Elbow heavier than the hand.” When pressing: “Force passes through the wrist, it does not stop there.”

Region 5 gate

  • In every posture the elbow is at least 2 cm below the wrist;
  • The wrist is never flexed beyond 20°;
  • Wall press for 5 s: no locked point, no pain.

Pass → region 6.
