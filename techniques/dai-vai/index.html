Shoulder Girdle — Sinking the Shoulders

Region 4: the shoulder girdle. A suspended structure attached only by muscle — why holding the shoulders is the root fault behind every arm fault.

Body MechanicsHead & NeckSpineThoraxShoulder GirdleElbow & WristWaist & PelvisKua & KneeFoot & AnkleThe Waist Program

Structure

The shoulder girdle is a suspended structure lying across the thorax. Its only bony attachment is at the front (sternum and first rib); the scapulae behind attach to the trunk by muscle alone.

That design gives the girdle far more freedom than the pelvis — and makes it easy to hold wrongly. Seven muscles per side control its position, rooted on the spine, head and ribs. The position of the spine, head and ribs therefore governs every arm movement.

The classical image: the girdle is the hilt of a sword, the fingers are the point. A crooked hilt never yields a true point.

Principle

沉肩墜肘 — sink the shoulders, drop the elbows.

“If the shoulders do not sink, the body from the chest up is imprisoned and the qi flows upward. If the elbows do not drop, force cannot be amplified and both sides of the chest lose their protection.” — Wu Chien-chuan, quoted in Complete Tai-Chi

“Both shoulders must be relaxed and hanging down. Especially avoid hunching them, otherwise the qi surges upward.” — Chen Xin, p. 658

Sinking the shoulders allows the girdle to hang. There is no “lowering” action — only ceasing to hold.

Common Faults

FaultSignCorrection
ShruggingShoulders above the ear-to-shoulder line; neck shortTen drops (drill 3); recheck after each posture
Rolled forwardBacks of the hands face backwards when arms hangExternally rotate the arms 10°; open the chest
Winging scapulaInner border lifts when raising the armReduce range; wall angel
Pinned girdle“Good” shoulder position but rigid, does not follow the armThe girdle must participate, not be clamped
Pressing down by forceTrapezius tight, fatigue after 2 minutesThat is holding, not sinking: release, do not press

Drills

DrillHowDosage
1. Scapular clockDraw circles with the shoulder blade: 12 → 3 → 6 → 9, slow, small.5 each direction
2. Wall angelBack and arms on the wall, slide up and down keeping the scapulae in contact.8 reps
3. Ten dropsRaise both arms to shoulder height, then let them fall completely. Feel the shoulders drop after the hands.10 reps
4. Two-minute hangStanding, arms down, attending only to heaviness at the shoulders. If they creep up, exhale and let them fall.2 min
5. External rotationArms hanging, turn the palms forward 10°, return. Chest stays un-thrust.8 reps

Cues & Gate

Cue: “Shoulders drop, don't press.” When the arms rise: “Shoulders still drop.”

Region 4 gate

  • Ear-to-shoulder distance ≥ 2 cm and unchanged when the arms lift to shoulder height;
  • 2 minutes with arms down: no trapezius fatigue;
  • No winging when pressing the arms forward.

Pass → region 5 (elbow and wrist), because the elbow can only be right once the shoulder is.
