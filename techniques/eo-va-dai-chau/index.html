Waist & Pelvis — Governor of the Whole Body

Region 6: waist and pelvic girdle. The centre of gravity lives here; this is the joint between the upper and lower halves.

Body MechanicsHead & NeckSpineThoraxShoulder GirdleElbow & WristWaist & PelvisKua & KneeFoot & AnkleThe Waist Program

Structure

The pelvis is the floor of the trunk: where all the weight above lands, and where the body's centre of gravity sits. It is therefore the centre of structural balance and of movement control.

Three large bones bound by nineteen ligaments into a bowl. Unlike the shoulder girdle, the pelvic girdle moves as a single unit — one side cannot move independently. Horizontal turning therefore happens on one hip joint.

Practical consequence: “turning the waist” is not twisting the lumbar spine; it is turning the pelvis-and-torso block on a hip.

Principle

腰為一身之主宰 — the waist is the sovereign of the whole body; 命意源頭在腰隙 — the source of intention is in the waist's hollow; 氣如車輪，腰如車軸 — qi like a wheel, waist like the axle.

“The waist is the governor of your whole body. Only when one can loosen and relax the waist can he keep his feet strong and make his lower body firm and stable.” — Yang Chengfu, Ten Essentials, item 3

“Relax the waist; make it light and loose… When the waist is relaxed and unfolding, it not only allows the chi to sink downward more easily… it also makes the lower part stronger and more solid.” — Wu Chien-chuan, Six Key Elements, quoted in Huang, p. 26

Important: immediately after these lines Huang substitutes “pelvis and hip joints” for “waist” and argues the statements then make mechanical sense. That is a modern reading, not the classical wording — cite it as his.

Cues & Gate

Cue: “Pelvis turns as one block, waist follows.” For shoulder-turners: “Shoulders are passengers.”

Region 6 gate

  • 10 turns of 45° per side, heels down, tailbone centred;
  • Brush Knee ×6 with the waist initiating at least 5 of 6;
  • No low-back pain after the session.

Pass → region 7. Fail → go straight to the 12-week program.
