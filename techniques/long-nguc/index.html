Thorax — Hollow the Chest, Raise the Back

Region 3: the thorax. Why a thrust chest sends qi upward and lifts the heels, and how to breathe without opening the ribcage.

Body MechanicsHead & NeckSpineThoraxShoulder GirdleElbow & WristWaist & PelvisKua & KneeFoot & AnkleThe Waist Program

Structure

The thorax is 37 bones joined at about a hundred places. Twenty-four ribs and the sternum form a round cage — in effect a suspended structure, carried by twelve thoracic vertebrae and the muscles linking it to the thoracic and cervical spine.

For mechanical balance the cage should hang naturally, as close to the spine as the structure allows. With so many movable joints it should move freely in breathing.

In most people it does not, because of one mistaken idea: that the chest must be lifted to look upright. The lifting muscles become tight and inelastic, and the breath turns shallow and fast.

Principle

含胸拔背 — “contain the chest, raise the back”.

“The chest should be contained and must not be thrust out. When contained, the qi sinks to the dantian. When thrust out, the qi surges into the chest, the upper is heavy and the lower light, and the heels float — a thing taboo to the martial practitioner. When the chest is contained the qi clings to the back, and strength issues from the spine.” — Chen Xin, p. 658

“The chest must not be thrust out; if thrust out, the qi crowds at the chest, the upper is heavy and the lower light, and the heels easily float up.” — Fu Zhongwen, Ten Essentials, item 2

This is the mechanical reading of “sink the qi to the dantian”: not that qi got heavier, but that the ribcage is no longer being pushed up.

Common Faults

FaultSignCorrection
Thrust chestLower ribs flare, sternum juts, lumbar over-archesClose the lower ribs like a door; do not slump
Collapsed chestChest concave, shoulders round, head forwardThat is collapse (塌), not containment: lift the sternum 3 mm
Upper-chest breathingShoulders rise on inhale, belly does not widenLateral rib breathing — drill 1
Breath holdingRed face, neck veinsCount aloud; exhale on the turn

Drills

DrillHowDosage
1. Lateral rib breathHands on the sides of the ribs. Inhale: ribs widen sideways, hands pushed out; shoulders stay down.10 breaths
2. Exhale, close the ribsExhale fully, let the lower ribs close and the lumbar spine lengthen. Hold 2 s, inhale.8 breaths
3. 90/90 ball hugSupine, calves on a chair, arms around an imaginary ball at chest height. Breathe into the back, not the front chest.2 min
4. Three-breath checkStanding, one hand on sternum, one on lower belly. Inhale: sternum hand rises ≤ 5 mm, belly hand widens.3 breaths, every session
5. Contain while liftingRaise both arms to shoulder height keeping the lower ribs closed. If the ribs flare, lower the arms.8 reps

Cues & Gate

Cue: “Chest in, ribs closed, back wide.” For a collapsed chest: “Hollow, but still tall.”

Region 3 gate

  • The three-point wall test survives a deep inhale;
  • 3 minutes standing: shoulders do not rise, no air hunger;
  • 20 steps: heels do not lift, no heaviness in the chest.

Pass → region 4. If the heels still lift, check region 8 first.
