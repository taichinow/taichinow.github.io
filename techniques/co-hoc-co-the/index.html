Body Mechanics — Map of Eight Regions

Eight structural regions of Tai Chi: head, spine, thorax, shoulder girdle, elbow-wrist, waist-pelvis, kua-knee, foot-ankle. One page each, one template: structure, principle, faults, drills, gate.

Why Learn Region by Region

Seen mechanically, the body is a living machine. The skeleton has three main structural units — head, thorax and pelvic girdle — joined by the spine and ideally aligned with the line of gravity. Two girdles carry the limbs, and the hands and feet are where force enters and leaves.

Every postural fault therefore belongs to one of eight regions. Fix it in the right region and one correction serves every posture; correct vaguely and each posture fails differently.

Every page in this series uses one template: structure → classical principle → common faults → drills with dosage → cues → a pass/fail gate. Read one page, practise two weeks, test, then move on.

The Order

The order is not arbitrary: it works down from the top (because the body hangs), then up from the ground (because force needs a root), with the waist in the middle as the joint between the halves.

1Head & neck虛靈頂勁The whole chain hangs from it
2Spine立身中正Load-bearing axis; cannot settle while the head juts
3Thorax含胸拔背Decides whether qi rises or sinks — i.e. top-heavy or not
4Shoulder girdle沉肩墜肘A suspended girdle; if held, spine and ribs lose freedom
5Elbow & wrist墜肘 · 坐腕 · 舒指Where force leaves; a consequence of region 4
6Waist & pelvis鬆腰 · 主宰於腰Joins the halves; the centre of gravity lives here
7Kua & knee松腰落胯Delivers force to the legs without loading the knee
8Foot & ankle其根在腳The root; without it the other seven are decorative
Suggested pacing: 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 (four weeks) · 5 → 6 (four weeks) · 7 → 8 (four weeks). Then return to region 6 with the 12-week waist program.

Five Shared Principles

These five recur in every region. Whichever one a region cannot meet is the region to work on.

Three Questions for Any Region

Ask three questions in this order before correcting anything. If the first fails, the other two are meaningless.

  1. Is it hanging? Is this region carried by gravity, or held by muscle? (Head, shoulder girdle, thorax, elbows are all suspended structures.)
  2. Is it aligned with the line of gravity? From the side: ear canal – shoulder – iliac crest – lateral malleolus.
  3. Can it move without losing structure? That is the working definition of sōng: joints open, tendons loose, bones keep the shape.
