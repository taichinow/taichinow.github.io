Kua & Knee — Open the Groin, Protect the Knee

Region 7: kua and knee. Where force descends into the legs without loading the knee — the most injury-prone region for practitioners over 50.

Body MechanicsHead & NeckSpineThoraxShoulder GirdleElbow & WristWaist & PelvisKua & KneeFoot & AnkleThe Waist Program

Structure

Kua (胯) is the groin crease — the fold between thigh and trunk, corresponding to the hip joint. The hip is a ball joint: it rotates in every direction, which is why every turning motion of the pelvis must happen there.

The knee is the opposite: flexion-extension, very little rotation, and it takes compression. The knee is not a turning joint. Any attempt to “turn with the knee” puts torsion into the menisci and ligaments — the commonest injury mechanism in a Tai Chi class.

Force rule: the leg drives the kua, the kua governs the body. Open kua, force passes through the groin to the foot; closed kua, force stops at the knee.

Principle

松腰落胯 (sōng yāo luò kuà) — relax the waist, drop the kua. The phrase appears intact in the Chen-style translation in the library.

“The waist is the key position governing the whole body's movement (腰為軸座 — the waist as the axle seat). The hip joint (kua) must relax and sink. With 松腰落胯 the two legs can root firmly and the force from the legs can pass through the waist to the hands.” — Chen-style Taijiquan Illustrated, Vol. 1

“The two legs are bent, and one must clearly distinguish empty and full… Otherwise the stepping is heavy and sluggish.” — Sun-style, p. 658

Common Faults

FaultSignCorrection
Knee past the toesKnee ahead of the toes when sinkingSit back with the hips, not down with the knees
Knee collapsing inwardKnees converge under loadPush the knee out over the second toe
Turning with the kneeFoot fixed, knee twists as the body turnsTurn at the hip; if needed, pivot the whole foot
Too low, too soonKnee below 90°, heels liftingRaise the stance for two weeks: “high but right”
Locked kneesLegs rigid straight in standingKeep 5° of bend at all times

Cues & Gate

Cue: “Weight through the groin, not the knee.” When sinking: “Sit back, don't sit down.”

Region 7 gate

  • Five sinkings: knees stay over the second toe, heels down, no pain;
  • Wall sit 30 s without shaking;
  • Single-leg 10 s each side, no hand support.

Pass → region 8.
