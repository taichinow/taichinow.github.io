The Unified Techniques Framework

A single coherent progression from Level 3.0 (beginner foundations) through Level 3.5 (intermediate integration) to Level 4.0+ (advanced internal mastery) — 70 topics, one map.

Why a Unified Framework?

The 70 techniques in this knowledge base were not written as a random catalog. They form a single, layered progression — each topic builds on the ones before it, each level unlocks capacities that the previous level could not access. Studying them in isolation is like reading sentences without understanding the grammar. This page provides the grammar.

The three levels are not separate arts. They are three depths of the same art, distinguished by what becomes automatic, what becomes conscious, and what becomes invisible.

  • Level 3.0 — Conscious incompetence becoming competence. You learn the form; you fight the form; eventually the form runs.
  • Level 3.5 — Competence becoming integrated. The form stops being a sequence and starts being a state.
  • Level 4.0+ — Integration becoming invisible. The form dissolves; what remains is the body's native intelligence.

The progression has a single underlying engine: structural alignment → energy awareness → energetic transformation → conscious dissolution of structure. Every one of the 70 topics lives somewhere on this single line.

The Core Engine: Four Phases of Mastery

Every skill in Tai Chi passes through four phases. Understanding the phase tells you what the practice is for, what to expect, and when to move on.

Phase 1 — Structure (Body learns the shape)

The body must learn to do what the mind already understands. This phase is dominated by micro-adjustments: where the knee is, how the spine stacks, whether the shoulder sits correctly. Energy is heavy, slow, mechanical. Mistakes are visible. This is Level 3.0 work — Peng, An, Sung, Three Principals. Twenty to thirty topics live here.

Phase 2 — Awareness (Energy learns the body)

The body can now perform the shape; attention shifts to what happens inside the shape. Breath, weight distribution, internal pressure, the dantian. The student notices what was previously invisible. Mistakes are still visible but the student now feels why. This is the bridge — Yi Dan Khi, 50+ Body, Target Point. Ten topics live here.

Phase 3 — Integration (Skill becomes one)

The shape, the energy, the breath, the mind begin operating as a single system. Movements stop being executed — they happen. The student can respond to novel situations. Mistakes become opportunities. This is the intermediate level — San Shou, Brush Knee, Twist Step, Rowing the Chariot. Ten to twenty topics live here.

Phase 4 — Transformation (Structure becomes invisible)

The student can produce any of the previous levels on demand, including the absence of structure. The body has internalized the form so deeply that it can let go of the form. This is advanced — Chan Si Jin, Fa Jin, Ting Jin, Iron Shirt, Yin-Yang Loop. Twenty topics live here.

Phase 5 — Dissolution (Skill returns to source)

The student can perform any movement or no movement and the energy continues to circulate. This is master level — Wu Wei, Spontaneous Movement, Merging Energy, Empty Void. The remaining ten topics live here.

The Structural Axis: How the Body Evolves

The body's relationship to structure changes through the levels. This axis tracks that evolution.

3.0 — Alignment as Effort

The body must be constantly reminded of structure. Posture breaks within minutes. Awareness wanders; shoulders rise; knees drift. The student practices maintaining structure. Key topics: Body Map, 50+ Body, Three Treasures.

3.5 — Alignment as Background

Structure runs without conscious effort. The student can focus on other things while structure holds. Posture survives an hour of practice. Awareness shifts to internal energy. Key topics: Brush Knee, Flowing Steps, Opening Eight Directions.

4.0 — Alignment as Choice

The student can choose to maintain structure or not, on a moment-to-moment basis. Advanced techniques often deliberately disrupt structure temporarily. The form becomes a menu rather than a sequence. Key topics: Collapse, Yin-Yang Integration, True Lock.

4.0+ — Alignment as Invisible Resource

Structure is so deeply embodied that it operates beneath conscious awareness. The student can move in any direction with full alignment without thinking about it. The form is a memory, not a constraint. Key topics: Six Harmonies, Root & Suspend, Empty Void.

The Energetic Axis: How the Internal Senses Develop

Three sensory capacities develop in sequence. Each opens access to the next.

3.0 — Proprioception

The student learns where their body is in space. Joints, weight, balance. This is the foundation of all internal practice. Key topics: Three Principals, 50 Tools, 20-Minute Home Practice.

3.5 — Breath-Mind Coordination

The student learns that breath is a tool that can be coordinated with movement. Not breath control — breath partnership. Key topics: Yi Dan Khi, Counter-Breathing, Target Point.

4.0 — Interoception

The student learns to sense the internal pressure, temperature, and flow of energy. This is sensing what is inside the body, not just where the body is. Key topics: Dantian, Breath & Energy Channels, Yin-Yang Loop.

4.0+ — Partner Sensation

The student learns to sense another person's energy through contact. Not what they intend — what they actually carry. Key topics: Listening Energy, Merging Energy, Hua Jin.

The Martial Axis: How Combat Awareness Evolves

Tai Chi is a martial art. The martial application of each level differs fundamentally.

3.0 — Static Defense

The student learns to hold a position against an applied force. Like a posture test: can the structure withstand? Key topics: Peng, An, Ji.

3.5 — Reactive Adjustment

The student learns to redirect incoming force through micro-adjustments. The form is alive, not held. Key topics: Twist Step, Counter-Step, Guardian Posture.

4.0 — Active Neutralization

The student can take the force in, transform it, and return it before the partner knows what happened. Key topics: Hua Jin, True Lock, Fa Jin.

4.0+ — Pre-emptive Movement

The student moves before force arrives, guided by the partner's nascent intention. Key topics: Listening Energy, Merging Energy, Fixed Stability.

The Mental Axis: How Attention Develops

The mind's role in practice shifts dramatically across levels.

3.0 — Mind as Commander

The mind issues instructions; the body obeys (or doesn't). Practice is about strengthening the command channel. Key topics: No-Mind, Empty/Full Body.

3.5 — Mind as Conductor

The mind orchestrates multiple simultaneous processes. The student learns to hold attention across many points. Key topics: 30-Minute Form, Grasp Sparrow's Tail Summary.

4.0 — Mind as Witness

The mind watches without commanding. Practice becomes observation. The student trusts the body to perform. Key topics: Wu Wei, What is Taiji.

4.0+ — Mind as Space

The mind is the environment in which practice happens, not the practitioner. Key topics: Empty Void, Spontaneous Movement, No-Mind.

The Three Pillars: Foundation, Structure, Expression

Every technique serves one of three purposes. A complete practice requires all three.

Pillar 1 — Foundation (Body's Relationship to Earth)

How the body connects to ground. Includes standing meditation, posture, alignment, footwork. Without foundation, nothing else holds. Key topics across levels:

  • 3.0: Three Principals, 50+ Body, Body Compression
  • 3.5: Twist Step, Emerging Step
  • 4.0: Root & Suspend, Ground Path, Fixed Stability

Pillar 2 — Structure (Body's Relationship to Itself)

How the body's parts relate to each other. Includes joint sequencing, spiral, six harmonies. Without structure, energy cannot transmit. Key topics across levels:

  • 3.0: Peng, Ji, An, Crossing the Threshold
  • 3.5: San Shou, Brush Knee, Opening Eight Directions
  • 4.0: Six Harmonies, Four Energies, Eight Trigrams, Five Elements

Pillar 3 — Expression (Body's Relationship to Energy)

How energy moves through and out of the body. Includes breath, dantian, fa jin. Without expression, the practice remains internal only. Key topics across levels:

  • 3.0: Yi Dan Khi, Three Treasures, Empty/Full Body
  • 3.5: Rowing the Chariot, Flowing Steps
  • 4.0: Dantian, Silk Reeling Energy, Fa Jin, Breath & Energy Channels

Level 3.0 — The Foundation Curriculum (30 Topics)

Goal: Build a body that can stand, breathe, and move without falling apart.

This level establishes the structural baseline that everything else depends on. The student learns the four energies (Peng, Ji, An, Lu), the basic breath-mind connection, the standing meditation, the form itself, and the common pitfalls of early practice. Without this level complete, higher levels cannot develop correctly.

3.0 Core Energies (4 topics)

  • Peng (Phòng) — The first line of defense, the foundational energy of all others.
  • Ji (Tỳ) — The hidden pull, the complement to Peng.
  • An (Án) — The grounding force, the closing of every action.
  • Crossing the Threshold (Xu Thúc) — The transition from form to intent.

3.0 Body Awareness (8 topics)

  • Sung (松) — Power without tension.
  • Sung vs Sui — The two ways of letting go.
  • Body Map — Joint-by-joint awareness.
  • 50+ Body — Adapted movement for aging.
  • 50 Tools — Daily body maintenance.
  • Three Principals — The prerequisites of root.
  • Body Compression — Finding space in crowds.
  • Shaking (Lay) — Drilling out stagnation.

3.0 Breath & Mind (5 topics)

  • Yi Dan Khi (Ý Dĩ Khí) — Mind-driven breath.
  • Three Treasures (Tam Bảo) — Jing, Qi, Shen.
  • No-Mind (Vô Tâm) — Moving without mental interference.
  • Embrace the Pillow (Ôm Thù) — Yielding as structure.
  • Empty/Full Body — The embodied paradox.

3.0 Form Practice (5 topics)

  • Slow is Fast — The speed-building paradox.
  • 30-Minute Form — The compressed curriculum.
  • 20-Minute Home Practice — Micro-sessions.
  • 5-Minute Reset — Recovery from desk work.
  • Twenty-Four Forms Overview — The complete path.

3.0 Philosophy & Pitfalls (8 topics)

  • What is Taiji — Foundational understanding.
  • Yin-Yang — The dynamic balance.
  • Empty/Full Transition — From void to reality.
  • Counter-Breathing — Breath under pressure.
  • Counter-Principle — Why opposite thinking works.
  • Seven-Layer Adjustment — Common breakdown points.
  • Target Point — Energy mapping.
  • Three-Movement Foundation — The core alignment.

Level 3.5 — The Integration Curriculum (10 Topics)

Goal: Move from executing the form to living in the form.

By this level, the student can perform the entire form with correct structure and basic internal awareness. The 3.5 work takes these components and weaves them together. Topics here focus on applications, transitions, and the moments between formal movements where the integration actually happens.

3.5 Application Topics (10 topics)

  • San Shou (散手) — Free-hand applications of form.
  • Brush Knee (Diân) — The universal adjustment movement.
  • Twist Step (Quay) — Redirecting power through the waist.
  • Rowing the Chariot (Khai Gôn) — The first real alignment of upper and lower.
  • Lifting the Paten (Tô Hô) — Supporting the unsupported.
  • Flowing Steps (Hoa Bước) — Neutral, not passive.
  • Opening Eight Directions (Bát Hướng) — Structure before reach.
  • Grasp Sparrow's Tail Summary — The four gates at a glance.
  • Guardian Posture (Thủ Bát Bảo) — Protecting your center.
  • Counter-Step (Nghịch Bước) — Adhere, stick, and yield.

3.5 Master Cue: "The form is not something you do; the form is something that happens through you."

Level 4.0 (Part 1) — Advanced Internal (10 Topics, Batch 6)

Goal: Discover that the form is a doorway, not a destination.

This level marks the transition from intermediate to advanced practice. The student begins to access the deeper layers — silk-reeling energy, internal listening, the dantian as a true source rather than a concept. Topics here are increasingly experiential rather than mechanical.

  • Silk Reeling Energy (Chan Si Jin) — The spiral of integrated power.
  • Wu Wei (Vô Vi) — The art of non-action in motion.
  • Dantian (Đan Điền) — The powerhouse below the navel.
  • Fa Jin (Phá Tấn) — Explosive power without muscle.
  • Listening Energy (Ting Jin) — Feeling before moving.
  • Iron Shirt (Cuồn Não) — Conditioning for impact absorption.
  • Yin-Yang Energy Loop — Circular power.
  • Central Equilibrium (Trung Nghị) — The middle way of balance.
  • Embrace the Pot (Ôm Nồi) — Full circle connection.
  • Breath & Energy Channels — The internal highway.

Level 4.0 (Part 2) — Advanced Integration (10 Topics, Batch 7)

Goal: Move from doing the form correctly to dissolving the form entirely.

Where Part 1 focused on accessing deeper layers, Part 2 focuses on letting those layers inform everything else. The student begins to see the form not as a sequence but as a vocabulary — words from which sentences can be built on the spot.

  • Six Harmonies (Lục Hợp) — Six-way integration.
  • Five Elements (Ngũ Hành) — Cycles of energetic transformation.
  • Eight Trigrams (Bát Quái) — Mapping internal directions.
  • Collapse (Sụp) — Deliberate surrender into structure.
  • Yin-Yang Integration — The neutral zone.
  • Root & Suspend (Neo & Treo) — Dual anchors of structural integrity.
  • Advanced Silk-Reeling Drills — Triple-plane spiral.
  • Spontaneous Movement (Tự Sinh) — When form dissolves.
  • Merging Energy (Hòa Nhập) — Becoming one with the push.
  • Ground Path (Đường Đất) — Full-circle force transmission.

Level 4.0+ — The Transformation Curriculum (10 Topics, Batch 8)

Goal: Make every skill invisible — the form becomes a memory, not a constraint.

This level represents the final transformation. The student can produce any skill from any previous level on demand. The form runs beneath conscious awareness. The student can teach, can partner-spar, can improvise — and the underlying structure holds even when nothing in particular is being performed.

4.0+ Power Transformation (4 topics)

  • Fixed Stability (Ding) — Unshakeable center.
  • Hua Jin (Hóa Tấn) — Transforming power into neutralization.
  • True Lock (Chéng) — Internal iron shirt against impact.
  • The Four Primary Energies — As unified skill.

4.0+ Spatial Intelligence (3 topics)

  • Circling the Moon (Trăng) — The hidden spiral.
  • Emerging Step (Khai Bước) — Mechanics of expanding stance.
  • Suspended & Heavy (Chanh) — Coordinating lightness and weight.

4.0+ Energy Mastery (3 topics)

  • Letting Go of Energy (Thiu) — Unwinding force.
  • Lift the Ridge (Cao) — Closing the gate at the top.
  • Empty Void (Thê) — The meaning beyond form.

The Progression Path — How Topics Connect

Most topics are not learned once — they are re-encountered at deeper levels. Here are some example progressions through the framework:

Path 1: The Structural Practitioner

3.0 Three Principals → 3.5 Opening Eight Directions → 4.0 Six Harmonies → 4.0+ Fixed Stability

Focus: structure → structure-in-action → structure-as-system → structure-as-choice.

Path 2: The Energy Practitioner

3.0 Yi Dan Khi → 3.5 Rowing the Chariot → 4.0 Dantian → 4.0+ Lift the Ridge

Focus: breath-mind coordination → breath in form → breath as source → breath as circulation.

Path 3: The Martial Practitioner

3.0 Peng → 3.5 San Shou → 4.0 Fa Jin → 4.0+ Hua Jin

Focus: structural defense → reactive adjustment → active expression → pre-emptive transformation.

Path 4: The Integration Practitioner

3.0 Sung → 3.5 Empty/Full Body → 4.0 Wu Wei → 4.0+ Empty Void

Focus: relaxation → embodied paradox → non-action → meaning beyond form.

Path 5: The Dissolution Practitioner

3.0 Twenty-Four Forms Overview → 3.5 Grasp Sparrow's Tail Summary → 4.0 Collapse → 4.0+ Spontaneous Movement

Focus: the form → the form's summary → the form's deliberate breakdown → the form's dissolution.

The Clinical Dimension — Why This Progression Works for the 50+ Body

Tai Chi is one of the most studied exercise systems in modern clinical research. The 3.0-3.5-4.0 progression aligns with documented physiological stages:

3.0 — Foundational Adaptation (Months 1-18)

Research shows measurable changes in: balance (Berg Balance Scale +6-9 points in 12 weeks), proprioception accuracy (+15-20%), resting blood pressure (-5 to -10 mmHg), and fall risk (-45%). The body is reorganizing its baseline.

3.5 — Systemic Integration (Months 18-36)

Studies document changes in: heart rate variability (+25% in parasympathetic tone), cortisol patterns (normalized diurnal rhythm), inflammatory markers (IL-6 down 20-30%), and sleep quality. The body's stress response begins to recover its flexibility.

4.0 — Structural Resilience (Years 3-7)

Long-term practitioners show: maintained bone density vs age-matched peers, slower gait speed decline, preserved reaction times, and 30-50% lower rates of cognitive decline. The body's structural intelligence has become permanent.

4.0+ — Adaptive Capacity (Years 7+)

Documented capacities include: rapid recovery from injury, emotional regulation under stress, and continued learning capacity. The body's response options have multiplied beyond what the original structure could support.

The key insight: the progression is not just faster — it is qualitatively different at each level. Each stage unlocks capacities that the previous stage could not access.

How to Use This Framework

If you are a 3.0 student

Begin with the four core energies (Peng, Ji, An, Xu Thúc) and the standing meditation foundations (Three Principals). Build the body map (Sơ Đồ Cơ Thể). Practice the form slowly (Chậm Là Nhanh). When body awareness becomes reliable, you are ready for 3.5.

If you are a 3.5 student

Use the application topics to test what the form actually does. Practice each application movement in isolation before combining. The 10 topics here are not a list to memorize — they are diagnostic tools to see what your form still needs.

If you are entering 4.0

The Part 1 topics (Batch 6) develop new sensory capacities. Do not rush. Each topic requires the previous ones to be settled. The Part 2 topics (Batch 7) integrate these capacities into a unified internal system.

If you are practicing 4.0+

The Batch 8 topics are about making everything invisible. Treat them as a self-check rather than a curriculum. The student who needs these topics still has work to do; the student who has integrated them stops needing the topics.

The diagnostic question

For every topic, ask: "At what level am I currently practicing this?" If a 3.0 student tries to practice 4.0+ skills, the body compensates and the skill becomes a bad habit. If a 4.0+ student stays at 3.0, the body underuses its capacity. The framework helps you match your level to the topic.

The Lifelong View

The 70 topics of this knowledge base are not a curriculum to complete. They are a map of a territory that, in principle, has no end. Each level opens questions that the previous level could not formulate. Each topic, when practiced at the right level, generates new questions that the framework itself does not answer.

After several decades of practice, a teacher may observe that the topics have become transparent — you no longer think of them as techniques but as descriptions of what is happening. The framework's highest use is to disappear.

The Chinese word for this state is wuji — no-thing, no-technique, no-framework. The framework exists to be transcended. When the student reaches the place where the framework is no longer needed, the framework has done its job.

"After many years of practicing the form, the form is forgotten. What remains is the body's natural movement."
